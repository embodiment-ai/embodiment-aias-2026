Distinguished Keynote Speaker USC

Dr. Antonio Damasio

Director, Brain and Creativity Institute

David Dornsife Chair in Neuroscience, Professor of Psychology, Philosophy, and Neurology · University of Southern California (USC)

World-renowned neuroscientist and author whose seminal research uncovered the neural substrates of emotion, feelings, decision-making, and consciousness. Pioneer of the somatic marker hypothesis and foundational works on homeostasis in cognitive architecture.

Antonio Damasio is Dornsife Professor of Neuroscience, Psychology and Philosophy, and Director of the Brain and Creativity Institute at the University of Southern California in Los Angeles. Trained as both neurologist and neuroscientist, Damasio has made seminal contributions to the understanding of brain processes underlying affect, decision-making and consciousness. His work has made a major impact in neuroscience, psychology, and philosophy. He is the author of several hundred scientific articles and is one of the most eminent psychologists of the modern era (see Damasio, A. in R. Sternberg, S. Fiske, D. Foss (Eds.), Scientists Making a Difference: One Hundred Eminent Behavioral and Brain Scientists Talk about Their Most Important Contributions, 2016). He is one of the most cited scientists worldwide. [Google Scholar H Index is 177; with 282,590 citations and Web of Science H Index is 103; with 60,700 citations].

Damasio’s recent work addresses the biology of consciousness, the role of life regulation in the generation of cultures, and the distinctions between Natural and Artificial Intelligence. Recent articles include Singer, J. and Damasio, A. (2025). The Physiology of Interoception and Its Adaptive Role in Consciousness, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences, 380(1939), Damasio, H. and Damasio, A. (2024). Sensing, Feeling and Consciousness, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 379(1908), https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2023.0243; Damasio, H. and Damasio, A. (2024) Homeostatic Feelings and the Emergence of Consciousness, Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience, 36(8), 1653-1659, doi: https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_02119; Damasio, H. and Damasio, A. (2023) A Joint Interview of Neuron Special Issue: Neuroscience unbounded, Vol. 111(21), 3337-3340; Damasio, A. and Damasio, H. (2023). Feelings are the Source of Consciousness, Neural Computation, (35)3, 277-286; Damasio, A. and Damasio, H. (2022). Homeostatic Feelings and the Biology of Consciousness, BRAIN, Oxford Academic, Vol. 145; 2231-2235; Carvalho, G. and Damasio, A. (2021). Interoception and the Origin of Feelings: A New Synthesis, BioEssays, https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.202000261; Vaccaro, A., Kaplan, J., & Damasio, A. (2020). Bittersweet: The Neuroscience of Ambivalent Affect. Perspectives of Psychological Science. 15(5), 1187-1199; Man, K. and Damasio, A. (2019). Homeostasis and Soft Robotics in the Design of Feeling Machines. Nature Machine Intelligence, 1, 446–452. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0103-7. Damasio’s books include Descartes’ Error, The Feeling of What Happens, Looking for Spinoza, Self Comes to Mind, The Strange Order of Things: Life, Feeling, and the Making of Cultures, and Feeling and Knowing, which are translated worldwide and taught in numerous universities. His new book, Natural Intelligence & The Logic of Consciousness (September 2026) has just been published. It is currently available in the US and UK original and in 16 translations worldwide. (10/26)

Damasio is a member of the National Academy of Medicine, the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and the Academia das Ciências de Lisboa. He has received numerous prizes, among them the Paul MacLean Award for Outstanding Neuroscience Research in Psychosomatic Medicine [2019], the International Freud Medal [2017], the Grawemeyer Award [2014], the Honda Prize [2010], the Prince of Asturias Prize in Science and Technology [2005], the Nonino Prize as Master of Our Time [2003] and the Pessoa Prize [1992]. He holds Honorary Doctorates from leading universities, some shared with his wife Hanna, e.g., the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne [EPFL], 2011 and the Sorbonne [Université Paris Descartes], 2015. In 2013 the Escola Secundária Antonio Damasio was dedicated in his native Lisbon. From 2016 to February of 2024, Damasio was a member of the Council of State of Portugal. In 2025 he received the Medal of Honor of the City of Lisbon. In 2026 he received The Public Value Award from Leipzig University.

Google DeepMind

Dr. Radu Soricut

Distinguished Research Scientist

Google DeepMind

Pioneering research leader in multimodal foundation models, situated AI, vision-language integration, and embodied machine intelligence.

Radu Soricut is a Distinguished Scientist at Google DeepMind, where he leads research on multimodal understanding, generation, and embodied reasoning. His current work focuses on advancing the visual, spatial, and physical reasoning capabilities of the Gemini family of models. His research contributed to foundational advances in vision-language modeling, large-scale multimodal datasets, and unsupervised representation learning, and includes widely adopted works such as Conceptual Captions, ALBERT, PaLI, RT, and Gemini.

IACS

Dr. Leonardo Christov-Moore

Senior Research Scientist & Neuroscientist

Institute for Advanced Consciousness Studies (IACS)

Leading investigator examining the neural basis of empathy, altruism, interoceptive awareness, and relational AI alignment.

I am a cognitive neuroscientist interested in the means by which self-maintenance and empowerment can beget empathy, trust, and regard for others, in both living and artificial systems. I and my colleagues have explored this topic via development of theory, agentic simulation, neuroimaging, multivariate analysis, neuromodulation, and studies of behavior and decision-making. My work has been supported by NIMH, Templeton World Charity Fund, Survival and Flourishing Fund, Softmax, and Tiny Blue Dot Foundation.

UCLA

Dr. Sahib Khalsa

Associate Professor & Psychiatrist

UCLA

Leading neuroscientist and clinician pioneering human interoception, body-brain communication, and visceral neural pathways.

Sahib Khalsa, MD, PhD, is an Associate Professor of Psychiatry and Biobehavioral Sciences at UCLA, where he leads clinical and neuroscientific research on interoception—how the brain senses, interprets, and integrates internal bodily signals. Using functional neuroimaging, physiological perturbation, and computational modeling, his research investigates visceral-neural pathways underlying emotion, anxiety, and self-awareness.

Univ. of Parma

Dr. Vittorio Gallese

Professor of Cognitive Neuroscience

University of Parma, Italy

World-renowned cognitive neuroscientist, co-discoverer of mirror neurons, and pioneer in embodied simulation theory across language and action.

Vittorio Gallese, MD, is Full Professor of Neuropsychology and Cognitive Neuroscience at the University of Parma and Director of the Laboratory of Social Cognitive Neuroscience. He is Adjunct Senior Research Scholar at the Italian Academy for Advanced Studies at Columbia University, New York, USA, and member of the Academia Europaea. Cognitive neuroscientist, he is one of the discoverers of mirror neurons. His research focuses on the relation between the sensorimotor system and social cognition by investigating the neurobiological grounding of intersubjectivity, psychopathology, language and aesthetics. He is the author of more than 300 scientific publications and six books.
